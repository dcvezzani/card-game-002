# Flexible table spec

Specs only. No implementation yet. The Rec Room styleguide stays the visual system. Work stays on `feature/ux-rework`.

## Locked so far

- The table is a place to organize decks, hands, piles, and chat. It does not enforce a card game's rules.
- A public window shares its position, size, and contents. Dragging or resizing it moves it for every player. Scroll and zoom are each player's own, on a public window and on a private one. One player scrolling or zooming does not move anyone else's view or change the shared frame. A new window opens at the default zoom. Zoom is saved with that player's scroll and restored when they rejoin.
- Every window sits on one desktop. Each player pans that desktop on their own. Public window positions stay shared in table space, so one player can pan over to a window without moving it for anyone else. That pan is saved and restored when they rejoin. The player list stays fixed on the screen and does not pan with the desktop. A small map of the desktop sits in the chrome beside the player list. It shows each window that player is allowed to see: every public window, their own private windows, and private windows they are invited to. It does not show anyone else's private windows. Clicking a window there pans only that player's desktop so the window is in view, and highlights it briefly on their screen. The click does not move or resize the window. Moving and resizing still happen on the desktop.
- Face-up and face-down belong to a deck, a pile, or the cards in a hand window. A private window may show the holder their own hidden cards. Making a window public does not reveal faces that stay hidden.
- The same entity can be referenced from more than one window. A control on the entity, Open in a new window, keeps the original in place and opens a private window on those same cards. Dragging an entity onto a window asks whether to move that view or also show it there. Moving a view takes it out of the source window. Also showing it leaves the source in place. A move inside a public window is seen by everyone. Each view can use its own drawing (fan, row, or grid). Only the owner can change fan, row, or grid on their own draw target. Any seated player can change the drawing of a dummy's draw target. Copy is separate and makes a new deck.
- Any seated player can edit a public window.
- The player who switches a window to private is the one who controls that view. Everyone else who had it open sees it cleared, with that player's name. The owner can invite players when hiding it, or later with an invite link.
- A window can become an entity, and an entity can become a window. Converting a window groups everything inside it into one new entity. You choose which window receives that group. Everyone sees the original window close and the grouped entity land there. Converting a window does not ask for confirmation, and undo is not offered. The hand window and the original public table still cannot be converted. A hand window cannot be packed into a group. Deleting a group removes the group. It does not delete a hand.
- An invited player can move, resize, and drag inside a private window the same as the owner.
- A card leaves a hand only when the player who holds that hand moves it. Any seated player can move cards in a deck or pile on the public table.
- Joining an empty table shows one public table window, a shared chat on that table, and a hand window for each player. Hands start empty. A player starts with no draw target.
- Moving one card at a time is the basic action. Shuffle, flip, deal, cut, and similar shortcuts sit on top of that. Any seated player can use those shortcuts on a public deck.
- Only the holder can delete cards in their own draw target. Someone who can see it still cannot. A dummy's cards can still be deleted by any seated player. Confirmation is unchanged. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can drop a card onto it. The card joins that target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Deal and Draw one are unchanged. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can drag cards out of it. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Dragging the whole target out of the hand window also clears it, and anyone who can see it may do that. A selection drag follows the same rule. Dragging a card from a face-down target onto empty canvas still asks face-up or face-down. A dummy's target is unchanged. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can use the shortcuts that object already has. A deck can be shuffled, flipped, dealt, drawn from, cut, and flip-topped. A pile can be shuffled and flipped. A single card can be flipped. Seeing it does not add shortcuts that object type does not have. Someone who cannot see the target cannot use those shortcuts on it. Shuffle is offered only when that target has at least two cards. It keeps the target's facing. Everyone who can see the window sees the new order. Draw one still puts the card into the draw target of the player who used it, so drawing from someone else's visible target takes a card from theirs into yours. Deal from that target sends its cards to the hands that were picked. A dummy's target is unchanged: any seated player can use its shortcuts. Any seated player can flip a deck or pile on the public table. Other objects in the hand window keep their own facing.
- The first shortcuts are shuffle, flip, deal, draw one, cut, and flip the top card. Any seated player can use them on a public deck.
- The first player to pick up a card locks it until they drop it. Another player cannot pick up that same card while it is held.
- The first deck a player can add is a standard deck. Adding it asks: 52 cards, ace through king in four suits, or that deck plus two jokers. The two jokers are different cards: one red and one black. Any seated player can add a deck.
- Deal asks for a count and for which players' targets. The deal list includes every seated player and every dummy, including a hand window the dealer cannot see. The count is only the cards in the draw target. No target means zero. Other cards in the hand window are not counted. Faces are not shown. The dealer still does not see the faces or that window. Each picked target receives that many cards from the top, in the order the targets were picked. The cards go to that player's draw target. If they have no target, the first card creates one in their hand window. If the deck runs out, the deal stops. Deal adds cards only to that target, not to every object in the hand window. Deal from a visible draw target that is a deck sends that target's cards to the hands that were picked.
- Copy asks each time: duplicate the cards the deck has now, in the same order and facing, or make a fresh shuffled deck of the same kind. A 52 stays a 52. A 54 stays a 54. The fresh deck does not ask about jokers again. The original deck is unchanged either way.
- A pile, a single loose card, and a grouped entity can be dragged between windows, along with decks. A hand window cannot be dragged into another window. The shared chat stays on the public table in this first spec.
- Dropping a card on another loose card makes a pile. Dropping a card on a pile adds it. A pile can be shuffled and flipped. Deal and cut stay on decks. Anyone who can see a draw target that is a pile can shuffle and flip it. Someone who cannot see it cannot. A dummy's target can be shuffled by any seated player. Shuffle is offered only when it has at least two cards.
- Cut uses a slider to choose the split. The cards above that point move to the bottom and keep their order.
- Converting a grouped entity back into a window follows the parent it sat in. A group in a public window opens as a public window. A group in a private window opens as a private window for the same people. Everyone who can see the parent sees the group open.
- A loose card or a pile dropped on a deck joins that deck, on top.
- Flip top lifts the top card off the deck and sets it beside the deck as a loose face-up card. That shortcut always sets the loose card face-up, including when the deck is face-down. The new top card of the deck is left as it was.
- Closing a window removes entities that were shown only in that window. An entity that still has another view stays in that other view.
- The owner of a private window, and any player invited to it, can close that window, except the private window that holds a player's own hand. That window is protected. Nobody can close it, including the owner and anyone invited. The context menu on that window does not offer Close, and it does not offer Delete for the window. The holder can still delete cards in that hand. Any seated player can close a public window other than the original public table. Closing still asks first and names what will be removed. The player who closed it gets the same ten-second undo. The original public table still cannot be closed, deleted, or converted. The context menu shows Close only when that player is allowed to close that window.
- The protected hand window can be made public. Only the holder can switch it between private and public, in either direction. No other player can, and that control is shown only to the holder. When the holder makes it private, everyone else loses sight of it, same as any window being hidden. The holder can invite people then, or later, and remains the owner. Invitees still cannot close it, convert it, or change its privacy. Faces stay on the hand, so while the hand is face-down that public view shows backs to everyone, including the holder. The holder's private face view is not this window once it is public. The window still cannot be closed, and it cannot be converted into a group, private or public.
- While that hand window is public, only the owner can move it or resize it. The frame is still shared, so everyone sees that placement. Other people cannot change it. Bringing that public window forward is also owner-only, and the new order is shared. It still cannot be closed or converted. Only the holder can delete cards in their own draw target. Someone who can see it still cannot. Anyone who can see it can drop a card onto it, and the card joins that target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Deal and Draw one are unchanged. Anyone who can see it can drag cards out of it, and can drag the whole target out of the hand window. That clears the target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Anyone who can see the target can use the shortcuts that object already has. Flipping that target does not flip a deck or pile sitting beside it. While the window is private, the holder and invitees can place objects there. While it is public, any seated player can place or move those other objects. A dummy's target is unchanged: any seated player can drag it out.
- Each person keeps their own private position and size for a window. Making the window private restores that saved private placement. It does not copy the public frame onto them. If they have never placed it privately, they get the default placement. Moving a window while it is private changes only that player's private placement. It does not change the saved placements of other players, and it does not move other private views. When the owner makes it public, the shared frame becomes that owner's current private position and size, including after they moved it while private. That is where it was just before the switch. Other players see that window jump to that place. Their saved private placement of this same window is kept and is not overwritten. Other private views do not move, resize, or change privacy. A separate private view of the same cards, such as one opened with Open in a new window, stays private, stays where it is, and does not become public. Making one view public does not reveal faces in those other views. Nobody's desktop pans. Scroll and zoom are not reset. The next time it is made private, each person returns to their own saved private placement, not to where the public window ended up. This applies to every window that can change privacy. A player's public hand window is the exception: only the owner moves and resizes it, and everyone sees that shared frame. Whether invitees can still place their own copy of a private hand window is not decided. The original public table still cannot be made private. Privacy authority is unchanged: only the holder switches the hand window, and only the owner makes any other private window public. An invited player cannot. When some other seated player makes an ordinary public window private, they become its owner, and people who are not invited lose sight of it.
- A hand is a special window, like the original public table. It cannot be deleted, closed, or converted. It cannot be dragged into another window and cannot be packed into a group. Cards in the draw target can be deleted. Only the holder can delete cards in their own draw target. Someone who can see it still cannot. A dummy's cards can still be deleted by any seated player. Confirmation is unchanged. No one else deletes cards in another player's target. Other objects in the window follow the usual delete rules. A hand window can hold other objects besides the draw target. Decks, piles, loose cards, and groups can sit there. The hand's cards sit on the same canvas as everything else in the hand window. There is no separate hand area. A player starts with no draw target. The first card from Draw one or Deal creates a face-down card on an open spot of that player's hand-window canvas, and that card becomes the target. Further cards join it. When a second card arrives, it becomes a pile and that pile stays the target. A deal of several cards does this with the first card, then adds the rest to that pile. If the target is later gone because its cards were deleted, the next incoming card creates a new target the same way. Each player has at most one target. That target is a single deck, a single pile, or a single card, and it sits in that player's hand window. A group cannot be the target. Draw one and Deal add cards only to that object. Other objects in the window are not targets. Only the holder can change their own target, and only to a deck, pile, or card already in their hand window. Any seated player can change a dummy's draw target, and that choice still has to be a deck, pile, or card already in the dummy's hand window. The previous target stays where it is. The draw target can be dragged out of the hand window. Anyone who can see it may drag the whole target out, and that clears it. Someone who cannot see it cannot. A dummy's target is unchanged: any seated player can drag it out. When it leaves the window, it stops being the target. Moving it around inside the window keeps it as the target. Dragging the last card out also clears the target. If at least one card remains, that object stays the target. The next Draw one or Deal creates a new face-down card in the hand window and that card becomes the target. Choosing a deck, pile, or card outside the hand window is refused. It does not move into the window. The context menu on an allowed object offers this choice. Everyone who can see the hand window can see which object is the target. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can use the shortcuts that object already has. A deck can be shuffled, flipped, dealt, drawn from, cut, and flip-topped. A pile can be shuffled and flipped. A single card can be flipped. Seeing it does not add shortcuts that object type does not have. Someone who cannot see the target cannot use those shortcuts on it. Shuffle is offered only when that target has at least two cards. It keeps the target's facing. Everyone who can see the window sees the new order. Draw one still puts the card into the draw target of the player who used it, so drawing from someone else's visible target takes a card from theirs into yours. Deal from that target sends its cards to the hands that were picked. A dummy's target is unchanged: any seated player can use its shortcuts. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can drag cards out of it. Someone who cannot see it cannot. A selection drag follows the same rule. Dragging a card from a face-down target onto empty canvas still asks face-up or face-down. A dummy's target is unchanged. Anyone who can see a player's draw target can drop a card onto it. The card joins that target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Deal and Draw one are unchanged. Deal can send cards to a hand window the dealer cannot see. The deal list includes every seated player and every dummy. The count is only the cards in the draw target. No target means zero. Other cards in the hand window are not counted. Faces are not shown. The cards go to that player's draw target. If they have no target, the first card creates one in their hand window. The dealer still does not see the faces or that window. While the target is face-down, the holder's private view of the hand window shows its faces. A public view shows backs. Other objects keep their own facing. Draw one does not pan their desktop. Cards can still leave the target when someone who can see it drags them. Open in a new window on those cards can still open another view. Open in a new window on another player's face-down hand shows backs. Only the holder's own private views show that hand's faces. A dummy's hand is the same kind of window while the dummy exists. Deleting the dummy still removes the dummy, that hand window, the avatar, and the list entry.
- While the hand window is public, only the owner can rename it, and everyone sees the name. Other people cannot change it. While it is private, the owner can rename it, and only the people who can see that window see the name. Whether invitees can rename it is not decided. Switching privacy does not reset the name.
- A player's hand window is special, and that player has permissions other viewers do not. When an action on that window or its draw target has not been explicitly shared, it belongs to the owner. Already shared with anyone who can see the target: dragging cards out, dropping a card on, and the shortcuts that object already has. Already owner-only: deleting those cards, choosing the draw target, switching the window between private and public, and, while the hand window is public, moving it, resizing it, renaming it, and bringing it forward. Only the owner can change fan, row, or grid on their own draw target. Any seated player can change the drawing of a dummy's draw target.
- Draw one moves the top card of a deck onto the draw target of the player who used it. If that player has no target, the card is created face-down on an open spot of their hand-window canvas and becomes the target. Drawing from someone else's visible draw target takes that card from theirs into yours. It does not pan their desktop. An empty deck does nothing. It does not add a card to the other objects in the hand window. Someone who cannot see that target cannot draw from it.
- Right-click on empty desktop opens that player's menu. On a touch screen, long-press on empty desktop does the same. The menu stays only on that player's screen and holds New window, New deck, and New dummy. The player list and the map do not hold these controls. Any seated player can use the menu. A new deck and a new dummy still ask the questions already decided. Shuffle, flip, deal, and the other entity actions stay on the entity.
- Right-click or long-press on a card, hand, deck, pile, window, or grouped entity opens a context menu for that object, only on that player's screen. The empty-desktop menu is unchanged and still only offers New window, New deck, and New dummy. The object menu lists only actions that player is already allowed to take on that object. It grants no new authority. If they can take no action, no menu appears. It shows Delete only when that player is allowed to delete that object, and Close only when that player is allowed to close that window.
- A new empty window starts private, owned by the player who created it. They can drag entities into it and later switch it to public.
- Adding a deck asks which window receives it. A deck placed in a public window is visible to everyone. A deck placed in a private window is visible only to the people who can see that window.
- A view can be drawn as a fan, a row, or a grid. A tight stack is not in the first spec. The drawing belongs to that view, so a public window shows one drawing to everyone, and another window can draw the same entity differently.
- A new deck, pile, or loose card starts as a row. Fan and grid are chosen afterward on that view.
- Delete removes that entity from every window. Every view of it disappears, and its cards are gone. A hand is not removed this way.
- Delete of a whole entity follows the same authority as moving cards. Any seated player who can see a deck, pile, loose card, or grouped entity can delete that whole entity. A loose card is still deleted as the whole entity by anyone who can see it. A hand window cannot be deleted this way. Deleting a group removes the group and does not delete a hand. While a player is away, others still cannot delete cards in that hand. Confirmation and the ten-second undo stay as they are.
- Deleting cards in the draw target leaves the hand window in place. Deal and Draw one still add cards to that target. This is true for a player's hand and a dummy's hand. Deleting the target's cards removes that target. Delete is not offered when there is no target. The next incoming card creates a new one. Confirmation names the cards that will be removed, as that player already sees them. Undo puts those cards back. Deleting a dummy still removes the dummy, that hand window, its avatar, and its list entry. That is separate from deleting cards in the target.
- The context menu on a card that is still inside a deck, pile, or hand can delete that one card. In a deck or pile, any seated player who can see it can delete that card. In your own hand, only you can. In a dummy's hand, any seated player can. In another player's hand, no one else can. The remaining cards keep their order. Confirmation asks first and names the card as that player already sees it. A face-down card is named as a back, and the confirm does not reveal its face to anyone who could not already see it. Undo covers that single-card delete for about ten seconds.
- One confirmation deletes every selected card that player is allowed to delete. If any selected card is not allowed, nothing is deleted. The confirmation names each card as that player already sees it, and a back stays a back. Undo restores that whole delete as one action, for about ten seconds. Remaining cards keep their order. A selection is still only cards in the same hand, deck, or pile. If the card whose menu was opened is part of the current selection, Delete applies to that selection. If it is not, Delete applies to that one card.
- A player who leaves or disconnects keeps their hand and private windows. Rejoining with the same name shows those windows again. Other players still cannot see the private windows.
- While that player is gone, a seated player can assume their seat. Public decks they placed stay where they are. When they rejoin before that, the control goes away.
- Assume ownership transfers the departed player's other private windows to the assumer. Invites stay. The assumer keeps their own hand window. Objects that were sitting in the departed hand window move into the assumer's hand window. They do not join the assumer's draw target. The departed cards that were in the draw target still merge into the assumer's draw target, after the assumer's cards, in the same order. They then follow that target. The departed hand window disappears with the seat. It is not kept as a second hand window. The assumer sees each transferred window where the departed player last had it. Invitees keep their own placements. The departed list entry and avatar go away. The assumer keeps their own avatar.
- The original public table stays public. It cannot be closed, deleted, or converted into an entity. Players can still move it, resize it, and change what is inside it.
- Delete, and closing a window that will remove entities, always ask first. The confirmation names what will be removed.
- A player can select several cards in the same hand, deck, or pile and drag them together. They land in that same order. The drag locks every selected card. If one of them is already locked by another player, the drag does not start.
- A chat log, a grid, or another entity can scroll inside itself. That inner scroll is local to each player. It does not move the window or anyone else's place in that log or grid.
- Each player can turn on a pointer from a toggle on that window. It stays on until they turn it off. While it is on, the window shows their name and a distinct color wherever they point. Several pointers can show at once. The pointer does not move cards or windows.
- A selection is visible to everyone who can see that window. The same cards are highlighted for all of them. A selection highlight on a player's own face-down hand is shared. Everyone who can see that window sees the same highlight. Faces stay hidden. Decks, piles, face-up hands, and dummy hands are unchanged.
- Dragging a card to a new spot in the same hand, deck, or pile changes that entity's order. Every view shows the new order.

- Public windows also share which one is in front. Bringing a public window forward brings it forward for everyone, except a player's public hand window, which only the owner can bring forward. That new order is still shared. Bringing a public window forward does not change which private window is in front on your screen. On your own screen, you choose which window is in front when a private window overlaps a public one, or overlaps another private window. That choice is only yours. It does not change the order anyone else sees.
- The table is saved. Leaving and a server restart both keep the windows, cards, chat, and each player's scroll in each window. A player who rejoins with the same name returns to the scroll they last left. A new arrival, or a window that player has not scrolled, opens at the default spot.
- A player is recognized by the name they type. If that name belongs to someone who is gone, the arrival gets that player's remaining private windows and hand.
- If someone has already assumed that seat, the name is free. The arrival gets a new empty hand window. The assumer keeps the cards that were merged into their hand.
- After that merge, those cards follow the assumer's hand.
- A name that someone seated is already using is refused. Two players cannot sit under the same name at the same time.
- A private window shared with invited players keeps its contents in sync, including card moves. Each invited player places, sizes, and scrolls that window on their own screen. A pointer marks a spot in the contents, so the others see it on the same item even when their window sits somewhere else.
- A grouped entity shows a small preview of what is inside, packed into one object. Opening it turns that group back into a window.
- A window or group starts with a default name, such as "Window 2" or the hand's owner. Anyone who can edit that window can rename it, except a player's hand window: only the owner renames it while it is public, and whether invitees can rename it while private is not decided. A public name is seen by everyone. A private name is seen by the people invited.
- Inside a window, entities sit on a free canvas. They can overlap and be dropped anywhere. Those positions are part of the contents, so everyone who shares the contents sees the same arrangement. A window scrolls when the canvas is larger than the frame.
- Dropping an entity, or clicking it, brings it to the front. That front-to-back order is part of the contents, so everyone who shares the window sees the same order.
- Fan, row, and grid are chosen from a control on a deck, pile, or card. Anyone who can edit the window can change it. A public window then shows the new drawing to everyone. Another window showing the same cards is unchanged. The hand's cards sit on the same canvas as everything else in the hand window. There is no separate hand area.
- After a confirmed delete, a close that removes entities, or a move that hides the last public view, the player who did it sees Undo for about ten seconds. It restores only that latest action. Another action replaces it. Using it brings back what was removed, and the other players see it return. Deal, Draw one, Shuffle, and Cut are not undone.
- A new deck starts face-down. While a draw target is face-down, the holder's private view of the hand window shows its faces. A public view shows backs. Other objects in the window keep their own facing.
- A new pile faces the same way as the loose card it was dropped on. Dropping a card onto a face-up card starts a face-up pile. Dropping onto a face-down card starts a face-down pile.

- Moving the last public view of an entity into a private window is allowed. You must confirm first, because other players will lose sight of it. Undo can restore that public view for about ten seconds.
- Anyone who can see a player's draw target can drop a card onto it. The card joins that target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Deal and Draw one are unchanged. They still add cards only to the target of the player who used them. Drawing from someone else's visible target takes a card from theirs.
- Dragging one card from a face-down hand onto an empty canvas asks: play it face-up, or set it down face-down. Face-up shows that card to everyone who can see the window. Dragging one card from a face-down deck or pile onto empty canvas asks the same prompt. The prompt applies only when the source is face-down. A card dragged from a face-up deck or pile onto empty canvas stays face-up.
- Dragging several cards from a hand onto an empty canvas makes one pile. One prompt asks whether that pile is face-up or face-down. All of the cards follow that choice. Dragging several cards from a face-down deck or pile onto empty canvas does the same: one pile, and one prompt sets that whole pile face-up or face-down. A pile dragged from a face-up deck or pile onto empty canvas stays face-up.
- Any seated player can write in the shared chat. Each message shows their name. The log is saved with the table and is still there after a restart.
- A dummy player stands in for a seat no person is using. A seated player creates a dummy, gives it a name, and it starts with an empty face-down hand window. That hand window is the same kind of window as a player's hand while the dummy exists.
- Seated players, dummies, and players who are away appear in one list beside the windows. The list is chrome: it stays put for each player, and it is not a window anyone drags or resizes. Each player scrolls it on their own. Everyone sees the same names and marks. Each has an avatar on the public table's canvas. Anyone seated can drag an avatar around that table, and the move is shared with everyone. An avatar cannot be moved into another window. A new avatar appears in an open spot on that canvas, and its position is saved with the table. A dummy's avatar and list entry are marked so it is clear that no person is connected. A dummy has no private window and cannot write in chat. The first person to drag an avatar locks it until they drop it. A player who leaves stays in the list, and their avatar stays on the public table, with an away mark different from a dummy's mark. Any seated player sees that mark, and assume ownership is offered on that entry. If they rejoin before a takeover, the away mark clears. If someone assumes that seat, that entry and avatar go away. The departed cards are merged into the assumer's hand, after the cards already there, and the assumer keeps their own avatar. Clicking a name scrolls only that player's view of the public table so the avatar is in view, and highlights the avatar for a short time on their screen only. Other players stay where they were and do not see the highlight. The click does not open a hand or reveal faces. Assume ownership stays a separate control on an away entry.
- A dummy's hand window stays public. It cannot be closed, deleted, converted, or dragged into another window. A real player's hand window is unchanged in this way: anyone who can see the draw target can drag cards out of it, and only that player's private views show its faces. Anyone who can see the target can still use the shortcuts that object already has.
- Any seated player who is not a dummy can open another public view, or their own private view, of the cards in a dummy's hand window and use that view to interact with those cards. They can share a private view the same way as any other private window. Card moves change the one shared hand, so every view updates. The private view is that real player's window: their own frame, size, and scroll. Fan, row, or grid on that view is theirs alone.
- Any seated player can move cards into or out of a dummy hand, flip it, and include it in a deal. A player's own draw target is flipped by anyone who can see it, using the flip shortcut that object already has. Anyone who can see the draw target can drag cards out of it and can drop a card onto it. The card joins that target. Someone who cannot see it cannot. Only the holder can delete cards in their own target. Someone who can see it still cannot. A dummy's cards can still be deleted by any seated player. Confirmation is unchanged.
- A face-down dummy hand shows its faces in each real player's own private view of that hand, to the player who opened it and to the people they invited. Bo sees the faces in his own private view even when he is not invited to Ada's. A view he is not in still shows backs, and the public table shows backs. Flipping the hand face-up shows the faces in every view. Flipping it face-down again returns the public table to backs. Each private view still shows faces to its owner and invitees. Switching a private view to public does not reveal the hidden faces. It becomes another public view and shows backs while the hand stays face-down.
- A dummy's name is reserved the same way as a seated player's name. Joining with that name is refused while the dummy exists, and the dummy keeps the name. A dummy cannot be given a name a seated player, a gone player who can still rejoin, or another dummy already has. Renaming the dummy, or deleting it, frees the old name. Deleting the dummy follows the usual delete confirmation and removes the dummy, that hand window, its avatar, and its list entry. That is separate from deleting cards in the hand, which leaves the hand window in place.
- A loose card has its own face-up or face-down state, separate from the deck or hand it left. Any seated player who can see it can flip it. While it is face-down, everyone who can see it sees the back.
